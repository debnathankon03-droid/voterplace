# Election Phases in India

## Overview
Indian general elections are conducted in phases, managed by the Election Commission of India (ECI). The process typically spans 6-8 weeks for Lok Sabha elections.

## Phase 1: Election Announced
- The ECI announces the election schedule, including dates for each phase.
- The **Model Code of Conduct (MCC)** comes into immediate effect.
- MCC restricts the ruling party from announcing new policies, making transfers, or using government resources for campaigning.
- The MCC remains in effect until results are declared.

## Phase 2: Nomination Period
- Candidates file their nomination papers with the Returning Officer.
- Each candidate must submit:
  - An affidavit with criminal records, financial assets, and educational qualifications
  - A security deposit (₹25,000 for General candidates, ₹12,500 for SC/ST candidates for Lok Sabha)
- Nominations are scrutinized by the Returning Officer.
- Candidates can withdraw their nominations before the last date of withdrawal.
- Final list of contesting candidates is published.

## Phase 3: Campaigning
- Begins after the final list of candidates is published.
- Candidates and parties campaign through rallies, door-to-door visits, media, social media.
- The ECI monitors campaign spending — limits are ₹95 lakh per candidate for Lok Sabha and ₹40 lakh for State Assembly.
- **Prohibited during campaigns**: appeals to caste/religion for votes, use of government machinery, bribing voters, hate speech.
- Campaigning must stop **48 hours before polling** (the silence period).

## Phase 4: Silence Period
- Starts **48 hours before the polling day** in a constituency.
- No campaigning of any kind is allowed — no rallies, no advertisements, no door-to-door visits.
- No opinion poll results can be published.
- Purpose: Give voters time to reflect and make an independent choice.

## Phase 5: Polling Day
- Voters cast their ballots at designated polling stations using EVMs.
- Multiple phases may cover different regions on different dates.
- Lok Sabha 2024 was conducted in 7 phases (April 19 – June 1, 2024).
- Typically polls are open 7 AM to 6 PM.

## Phase 6: Counting Day
- Happens on a single designated day after all phases of polling are complete.
- Counting occurs at designated counting centers under CCTV surveillance.
- Postal ballots are counted first.
- EVM results are tallied round by round, constituency by constituency.
- VVPAT slips from 5 randomly chosen booths per constituency are matched against EVM counts.
- Results are announced as they come in; trends are available on the ECI website.

## Phase 7: Results & Government Formation
- The candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins (First Past the Post system).
- A party/alliance needs 272+ seats (simple majority) to form the government in Lok Sabha (543 seats).
- The President invites the leader of the majority party/alliance to form the government.

## Key ECI Bodies
- **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)**: Heads the ECI.
- **Election Commissioners**: Two additional commissioners form the full commission.
- **Returning Officer (RO)**: Manages elections in a constituency.
- **District Election Officer (DEO)**: Manages at the district level.
- **Booth Level Officer (BLO)**: Assigned to each polling station for voter roll maintenance.
